01 · THE CASE

The AI data center of 2035 belongs in orbit

On Earth, the power runs out first. A few hundred kilometres up, the Sun almost never sets.

58%of 2035's users is all Earth's 500 MW grid can serve
8×more sunlight per solar panel a year than on the ground
4×less energy per chat answer than a 2025 Gemini prompt

58%: Earth-only run, designs/A+B+C-earth-only.json · 0.054 Wh (Design 2) counts whole racks, idle time and cooling; Google's 0.24 Wh is its median Gemini text prompt, May 2025

02 · WHY SPACE

What orbit has that the ground doesn't

On EarthIn orbit
PowerGrids are full: Texas has ~474 GW of big new loads asking to connect No grid. A dawn-dusk orbit sees the Sun almost all year
CoolingEvaporative cooling uses 1.8 L of water per kWh (slide 14)Radiators shed heat to −53 °C space; no water, no land
Cost trendBuilding cost rose $7.7M → $10.7M per MW, 2020–2025 Launch heading from ~$2,700/kg to ≲$200/kg by the mid-2030s
Reach25–110 ms round trip from our sites40 ms to everyone, from overhead (slide 12)

The catch: a megawatt in orbit only costs the same as the cheapest Earth site once satellites get lighter. That is Design 2.
