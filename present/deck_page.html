The AI data center of 2035 belongs in orbit

On Earth, the power runs out first. A few hundred kilometres up, the Sun almost never sets.

58%of 2035's AI users is all Earth's 500 MW grid can serve
8×more sunlight per solar panel each year than on the ground
4×less energy per chat answer than a 2025 Gemini prompt

Our orbit  dawn-dusk sun-synchronous, ~650 km, 98° tilt · 12 rings of 8 MW satellites

58%: Earth-only run, designs/A+B+C-earth-only.json · 0.054 Wh (Design 2; 0.049 Wh Design 1) counts whole racks, idle time and cooling, on the organizers' 2035 chips; Google's 0.24 Wh is its median Gemini text prompt, May 2025
Why space

What orbit has that the ground doesn't

Power
EarthGrids are full. Texas alone has ~474 GW of big new loads asking to connect OrbitNo grid. A dawn-dusk orbit sees the Sun almost all year
Cooling
EarthEvaporative cooling uses 1.8 L of water per kWh (organizers' slide 14) OrbitRadiators shed heat to −53 °C space. No water, no land
Cost trend
EarthBuilding cost rose from $7.7M to $10.7M per MW, 2020–2025 OrbitLaunch is heading from ~$2,700/kg to ≲$200/kg by the mid-2030s
Reach
Earth25–110 ms round trip from our sites to users Orbit40 ms to everyone, from overhead (organizers' slide 12)

The catch: orbit isn't free. A megawatt in orbit only costs the same as the cheapest site on Earth once satellites get lighter. That is our Design 2.

Biggest saving

Background agents use the live-agent racks in quiet hours: $4.8B saved. The world is below average demand 13 hours a day.

Why it holds

Many small places instead of a few big ones. One outage takes out a small slice, so the backup is small.

Before we fixed it

Losing a big orbit section dropped 37% of live users. Spreading over 12 rings cost about $3.0B.
