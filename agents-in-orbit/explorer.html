Agents in Orbit

50 million agent developers in 2035, served entirely from low Earth orbit: no Earth sites, no 500 MW grid cap, sunlight for power and deep space for cooling. The 40 ms round trip sits inside the 2 s time-to-first-token, so interactive steps fly too; their racks carry a full-load eclipse battery while background racks simply pause. A digital twin of the orbital data centre: PUE built from components, eclipses from the orbit geometry, a year of diurnal demand simulated hour by hour, and the levers the slides leave open swept for the fewest joules per task. The Earth design stays in as the reference.

Assumptions

From the slides: Kimi K3 (2.8T total, 104B active, 14 KB KV per token + 217 MB state), Qwen3.5-122B (122B/10B, 12 KB + 75 MB), 2035 GPU rack of 144 chips, 3 TB at 120 TB/s (72 usable), 792 kW, $23.8M; 256k context with 250k cached, 500 tokens out, 80 steps per task; interactive 100 tok/s, background 30 tok/s; peaks 2.0x and 1.2x the daily average. Assumed (replace with the starter catalog): 150 PFLOPS per GPU at 50% utilisation for prefill, 4-bit weights shared by 8 chips, Earth PUE 1.15 with direct liquid cooling, orbit PUE 1.05 in a dawn-dusk sun-synchronous orbit (99% sun), arrays at 300 W/kg, two-sided radiators at 1 kW/m² and 5 kg/m² and $1,000/m², $2M bus per rack, 3 t bare rack, 1.5x structure mass factor, 10 s tool wait for background steps. Added after the design review (all assumed): one 100 kW CPU/NVMe sandbox rack per GPU rack (1.5 t, $2M) because ~170k background tasks run tool calls against each GPU rack; 600 kg of 5 mm-Al-equivalent shielding over HBM and power electronics ($100/kg); a 30 kWh housekeeping battery (250 Wh/kg, $300/kWh) and a 0.99 capacity factor because background work pauses during the ~1% of the year in eclipse instead of flying a GPU-sized battery; 350 kg and $1M of electric propulsion per rack for drag make-up and end-of-life deorbit under the 5-year rule; 150 kg and $1.5M of laser inter-satellite and relay/gateway terminals; 14% Earth-IR derate on the Earth-facing radiator face (7% more panel area). All in space (assumed): interactive-agent racks cannot pause, so they carry a battery for the whole rack through the longest 35 min eclipse of a 550 to 600 km dawn-dusk orbit (576 kWh, 2.3 t, 28.2 t per rack launched) instead of the 0.99 throttle; the Earth design stays in the model as the reference. Derived, not assumed: PUE is built from components. Orbit: array regulator 98.5%, HV harness 99%, rack converter 98% (4.6% together), ammonia two-phase loop pumps at 200 kPa (0.1%), 2 kW of avionics, gyros and laser terminals per rack (0.2%): PUE 1.050, 1.054 on battery racks. Earth: UPS 96% and PDUs 98.5% (5.8%), CDU pumps and dry coolers 8%, lighting and security 1%: PUE 1.148. Eclipses come from the orbit geometry: 575 km, 97.6° inclination, 06:00 local time of the ascending node; beta angle falls to 59° in June, so eclipses up to 21 min of a 96 min orbit on 96 days a year, 4.6% of the year. Background racks are sized so the worst eclipse day still clears its work (x1.067); interactive battery = longest eclipse + 20%, 26 min. The year simulation runs hourly with a diurnal cosine demand at the slides' peak factors; idle racks draw 30% (GPU) and 50% (sandbox) of full power. Second pass (all assumed): the same sandbox rack on Earth, plus Earth ops at 1%/yr of capex; in orbit 1.5 kg/kW of power conditioning and harness, 750 kg of pumps, fluid, cold plates and a redundant loop per rack, 50 kg of attitude control per rack, arrays sized for 85% end-of-life output, ops at 2.5%/yr of hardware capex, and $1.5B of spacecraft NRE spread over 270 racks. Joules per task = GPU-seconds (decode shared by the users one GPU serves, plus prefill) × 5.5 kW per GPU, plus the sandbox slot's share of its 100 kW rack over the task's wall time (one of ~170k slots), × the site PUE: 1.15 on Earth with direct liquid cooling, 1.05 in orbit (2.5% power conversion, 1% pumps, 1.5% avionics and comms).
