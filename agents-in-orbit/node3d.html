Bill of materials, one node

Design choices

Orbit. Dawn-dusk sun-synchronous, 550 to 600 km: the node rides the day-night terminator and sees the sun almost continuously. During the ~1% of the year in eclipse a background node throttles its workload instead of carrying a GPU-sized battery; a 30 kWh housekeeping battery keeps avionics, loop pumps and sandbox state alive. The orbit geometry (575 km, 06:00 node) gives eclipses up to 21 min of a 96 min orbit on 96 days a year. An interactive node cannot pause, so each of its racks carries a 436 kWh battery (1.7 t) for the longest eclipse plus 20%: 27.4 t per rack instead of 24.9 t. At night the interactive nodes also run the queued background work, which is what shrinks the fleet to 261 racks. Round trip to a ground gateway is 40 ms, far inside the 10 s background-agent budget.

Attitude. Spine along the velocity vector. Arrays lie in the plane normal to the sun. Radiators are rotated 90° so sunlight only grazes their edge, and both faces radiate to 3 K space.

Thermal path. Chips on cold plates, a pumped two-phase loop along the spine, manifolds at each truss end feeding the radiator wings at about 70 °C. No air anywhere: the 2035 rack is already direct-liquid.

Racks. Sealed, conduction-cooled boxes on the shaded face of the spine, 20% spare chips for radiation and the absence of repair, 600 kg of 5 mm-Al-equivalent shielding over the HBM stacks and power electronics. No pressurised volume, no crew access.

Sandbox. Each GPU rack carries a 100 kW CPU/NVMe box: ~170k background tasks run their 80 tool calls per task in mostly idle microVMs that have to sit next to the model, not 40 ms away on Earth.

Power and thermal hardware. One power-conditioning box per rack on the spine's shaded flank (1.5 kg per kW), supply and return lines of the two-phase loop under the racks, redundant pump packs on each radiator manifold, and a solar array drive with deployment hinges at each boom root. Arrays are sized for 85% output at end of life.

Attitude and assembly. A four-gyro CMG cluster near the centre of mass and two star trackers on the avionics box hold a 400 m structure sun-pointed against gravity-gradient torque. The ring on the +Z face is the berthing fixture: launch one is the spine with racks, launch two berths the wing and radiator package there.

Comms and propulsion. Ring laser terminals sit on the booms 6 m off the spine so they look past the radiator wings; the gateway terminal hangs under the racks and looks nadir beneath the array plane. Two Hall-thruster pods ride the booms 11 m out, canted 20° outboard so their plumes clear the aft radiator, with a krypton tank in the truss: ~200 m/s over 5 years for drag make-up and the end-of-life deorbit the 5-year rule needs.

Launch. Wings fold flat along the spine; the stack fits a 9 m fairing. Each node of 8 racks is two 100 t launches at $200/kg, about $5.0M per 24.9 t rack (15 t and $3M before the review items). The all-in-space fleet for 50M developers is 261 racks (120 interactive with batteries, 141 background) in 33 nodes, about 6.6 kt and 67 launches once background work is pooled onto the interactive nodes at night.
