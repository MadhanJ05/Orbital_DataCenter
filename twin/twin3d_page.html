How to use it

A live twin of our planned fleet

This is our planned 2035 fleet placed in the real world right now: today's Sun and Earth, every satellite on its own dawn-dusk orbit, and the Python twin's fleet model running in this page.

  • Look around: drag to turn, scroll or pinch to zoom, double-click to reset the view.
  • Inspect: click any satellite or Earth data center. The panel on the right shows its state now; Follow rides along with it.
  • Break it: pick a site or plane under Failure drill, or press Switch off on the selected one. The rest of the fleet takes its users at once.
  • Time: the dock runs time at ×1 (real time) up to ×600, and jumps to 21 June or 21 December, when each half of the fleet is in eclipse season.

How it works

Checking this page's model against the Python twin…

Positions come from real orbital mechanics (circular orbits at 650 km, sun-synchronous at 97.99°) and the real Sun for the time shown. The fleet's load, background queue and power come from the Python twin's minute-by-minute model (twin/simulate.py), run here from two days back by twin/twin3d_engine.js. On every load this page re-runs every day the Python twin simulated and checks it gets the same answers; node twin/check_twin3d.js does the same from the command line.

Our placement choices, which the model doesn't fix: each group's six planes fan out by 4 minutes of local time, and the 16 ground stations sit at existing optical, observatory and polar ground-station sites, linking above 20° of elevation. Earth and orbits are to scale; satellites, sites and stations are drawn far larger than life. Earth: NASA Blue Marble and Black Marble.
